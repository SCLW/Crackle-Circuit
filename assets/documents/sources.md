## STEIM and the Cracklebox

STEIM (Studio voor Elektro-Instrumentale Muziek) was founded in Amsterdam in 1969 by Misha Mengelberg, Louis Andriessen, Peter Schat, Dick Raaijmakers, Jan van Vlijmen, Reinbert de Leeuw and Konrad Boehmer. For half a century it was one of the most important centres in the world for the research, development, and performance of new electronic and electro-instrumental music. Michel Waisvisz, the inventor of the Cracklebox, served as STEIM's artistic director from 1981 until his death in 2008.

STEIM ceased to exist as an organisation at the end of 2020, after the Dutch arts council ended its structural funding following years of budget reductions. Its closure marked the end of a uniquely hands-on, instrument-builder culture in Dutch electronic music.

For a thorough academic treatment of STEIM's decades of instrument research, see Andi Otto, *The STEIM Touch: The Emergence of Musical Sensor Instruments* (Goldsmiths Press, 2024), drawn from extensive primary-source research in STEIM's archives, including Michel Waisvisz's papers.

## John Richards, Dirty Electronics

John Richards's Dirty Electronics project (Leicester, UK, since 2003) is the most active contemporary lineage running parallel to the Cracklebox. Richards has built and taught a long series of touch-controlled DIY noise instruments around different cheap ICs, including a build around a CMOS hex inverter (the 4049, used as a chaotic oscillator in the Forrest-Mims tradition) and another around the LM358 dual op-amp. Both are excellent points of comparison for this design.

- Dirty Electronics: <https://www.dirtyelectronics.org/>
- John Richards on Wikipedia: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Richards_(musician)>
- Interview, *ATTN:Magazine*: <https://www.attnmagazine.co.uk/interview-john-richards-dirty-electronics/>

## Note on the original Crackle Pack guide

The scanned Crackle Pack guide previously bundled in this folder was a STEIM-issued pamphlet accompanying the historical Cracklebox kit. It has been removed from the repository pending explicit redistribution permission. With STEIM closed since 2020, the right path for anyone wishing to reproduce or consult the original pamphlet is to contact people who were involved with STEIM and Michel Waisvisz directly.
